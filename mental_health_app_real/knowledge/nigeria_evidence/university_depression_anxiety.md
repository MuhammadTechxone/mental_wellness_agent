# Prevalence and Correlates of Depression and Anxiety Among Nigerian University Undergraduates

## Summary of Findings
Depressive and anxiety symptoms are prevalent among Nigerian university students, with observed rates ranging from 21.2% to 28.5%. Key contributing factors include:
- Severe academic workload and examination anxiety
- Financial hardship, delays in allowance, and inflation impact
- Irregular academic calendars due to strikes and systemic disruptions
- Fear of post-graduation unemployment and societal pressure
- Interpersonal friction in crowded campus hostel accommodations

## Cultural Idioms of Distress (Somatization)
Somatization is frequently the primary mode of expressing emotional distress among Nigerian students rather than overt psychological disclosures. Common somatic complaints include:
- *"Head is hot"* (chronic tension headaches, cognitive overwhelm)
- *"Body is doing me somehow"* (generalized anxiety, autonomic arousal)
- Generalized bodily weakness and unrefreshing sleep

## Clinical Implications
Early identification of sleep disturbances, concentration difficulties, and loss of interest in peer engagement can prevent academic failure and functional decompensation. Interventions should prioritize academic flexibility, sleep hygiene, and destigmatized counseling.
