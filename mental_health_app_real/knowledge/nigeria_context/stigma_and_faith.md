# Sociocultural Context: Mental Health Stigma, Family Expectations, and Faith in Nigeria

## 1. Stigma and Help-Seeking
Mental health conditions in Nigeria remain heavily stigmatized. Psychological vulnerability is frequently mischaracterized as spiritual weakness, lack of prayer, moral failure, or family curse. Consequently:
- Individuals often conceal symptoms until severe functional decompensation occurs.
- Primary care clinics or spiritual houses are often visited before seeking psychological or psychiatric consultation.
- Communication should be non-judgmental, emphasizing confidentiality and normalizing emotional struggles.

## 2. The Role of Faith and Community
Spirituality, prayer, and community support (Ubuntu: "a tree does not make a forest") serve as potent protective factors in Nigerian culture. Psychotherapeutic interventions must respect religious beliefs while gently disarticulating clinical symptoms from personal spiritual adequacy. Clinicians should foster collaboration between professional mental healthcare and healthy community/faith practices.
